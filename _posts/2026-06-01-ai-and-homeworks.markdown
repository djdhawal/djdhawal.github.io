I seem to have lucked out by being enrolled in graduate education at the time of a shape shifting technical landscape. As I work through problem sets, homework assignments, and final projects, I have come to realize that neither the rubrics nor the homework design is able to keep up with the ways of the times.

Whats the problem:
Homeworks are intended to be a check if you’re keeping up with the course material effectively or not and I don’t think in today’s times conventional homeworks are able to replicate that environment. In lieu of the abilities of sota llms, I haven’t come across any homework problem in the recent past which isn’t a single shot generation problem for any free tier llm.
Every student is constantly juggling a social life, part time work to pay their bills and the hell hole of job applications. Solving homeworks in a single shot enables you to allocate much more hours to other stuff and also get decent grades. The objective maximises high GPAs which can be sustained without actually learning the material. Students now have a chance of skipping the dilemma of compromising a social life for studies.

Everyone already realizes that GPAs are becoming a more irrelevant metric of intellectual and cognitive capabilities. I think the only reason someone might even barely want to give learning stuff a chance, would only be if they genuinely care about the subject, like the language of the subject, value the knowledge they will extract. Things definitely do not change for people who like to learn and think that education holds more value than just making you a memory machine or a retrieval system. People who believe that learning something will alter the way they think, then they might actually be able to learn, apart from completing assignments.

What I propose:
1. Homeworks should present real problems, not toy tasks
2. They should be done in a shared space with a group of people, with a command structure, established by professors (something like enterprise tech teams)
3. Homeworks should be done in a class (won't be called homework then though), Grading should be instant.

I believe that this would work in two ways. One, solving real problems would enable AI assisted learning. It can really make a student smart about what to retain while building something and also what to leave for ai to figure out. Two, working in groups makes you aware of the depth of knowledge of the team, and you are able to realize the gaps that ai wouldn’t be able to fill in your knowledge more vividly. Professors/instructors can help more here, by sharing stuff from their experience which might not be natural to ai generated answers, or might be something that ai would often get wrong. 

This would have a benefit of the keeping the classroom on the same page about AI use, improvement in its abilities as it evolves. This should gain more importance as it’s easiest to lose track and not exploit the full potential of this technology. Its like have having a 1GB/s wifi connection and using that to only read messages. 

In fact, this method of delivering the material can enable improved inter-disciplinary learning. Econ and math classes can pair up and problems at their intersection can be tackled in a group homework. This could lead to a more general learning and a sense of developing ideas which is more holistic.
